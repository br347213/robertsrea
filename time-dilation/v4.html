How Can Time Actually Run Differently?
This is the question

“If time is time, why wouldn’t your second always equal my second?”

Forget the age calculator for a minute. The goal here is to show what is physically different.

Key idea: there is no invisible master clock behind the universe. A clock measures how much time passes along its own motion through spacetime. When motion changes, the spacing between that clock’s ticks can change relative to someone else.
01 · See time stretch

Same start. Same reunion. Fewer traveler ticks fit in between.

Drag the speed. The two vertical rulers cover the same Earth interval. Watch the traveler’s seconds spread farther apart.

At 0%, both rulers match. Near light speed, one traveler-second occupies more Earth-time.

What you are looking at: both clocks start together and end at the same reunion. Earth fits 10 local seconds in the interval. At 80% of light speed, the traveler fits only 6. That is what “the traveler ages less” physically means: fewer local physical ticks occur along that path.
02 · Why must those ticks spread out?

Because light refuses to change speed.

This is the mechanism. Build a clock out of a light pulse bouncing between two mirrors.

The two yellow dots move at the same speed. Only the path changes.
Moving light path1.67× longer
Same light speed. Longer distance. The moving mirror slides sideways while the light is traveling. The light has farther to go, but it cannot go faster. So the next tick happens later in Earth’s frame. That is the physical reason the traveler’s clock ticks are farther apart.
03 · Why does this include your body?

Your body is just billions of tiny clocks.

A heartbeat, atom, nerve signal, chemical reaction, and cell division are all physical changes happening over time.

But inside the spaceship…

Everything still feels completely normal because all of those processes agree with each other locally.

Your heart does not feel slow.
Your watch still says “one second” after one local second.
Your brain does not notice anything.
Thoughts, chemistry, metabolism, and clocks all stay synchronized with one another.
There is no hidden master clock in your body.
So there is nothing inside you saying “I should have aged more by now.”
04 · The big mental shift

There is no universal clock behind both lives.

The universe does not supply one master stopwatch that both people must match.

Same local second

Each person’s own second always feels like one second.

Different spacing between ticks

Relative motion changes how those local seconds map onto the other person’s frame.

Different total at reunion

Start together, meet again, compare clocks: the totals can differ.

05 · See the whole trip

Same departure. Same reunion. Different number of life-ticks.

Both people were living normally the entire time. Neither person felt slowed down.
The difference is the number of local physical ticks between the same two shared events.
That is what “aging differently” actually means. Not a visual illusion. Not a feeling. A different amount of physical change happened along each path.
Still weird? Good.

The two questions that usually remain.

But shouldn’t there still be one true time somewhere?

That is exactly the assumption relativity removes. Physics has never found a universal master clock or universal present. What clocks measure is local time along their path through spacetime.

If the traveler sees Earth moving too, why isn’t Earth the younger one?

During steady motion, each can describe the other clock as slow. The full round trip is not symmetric because the traveler turns around and changes motion. When they reunite, both clocks are side by side again and both observers agree on the final readings.

The visual to remember: same start and finish, but the traveler’s local ticks are spaced farther apart in Earth’s frame, so fewer of them fit in between.
